### 1. `contracts/ConsentRegistry.compact` ✅

**Purpose**: Privacy-first LGPD/GDPR consent management on Midnight Network.

**LGPD compliance coverage**:
- Art. 7 (legal bases) — consent must be the explicit legal basis
- Art. 8 (consent requirements) — specific, informed, unambiguous
- Art. 8 §5 (revocation) — revokeConsent circuit, as easy as granting
- Art. 8 §6 (policy change re-consent) — policy_version field + updateConsentPurposes circuit

**Architecture decisions**:
- `subject_id: Bytes<32>` = sha256/keccak256 of subject identifier → **no PII on-chain**
- `purposes: Uint<8>` bitmask: bit 0=essential, 1=analytics, 2=marketing, 3=third_party, 4=profiling
- `policy_version: Uint<8>` → enables re-consent workflow when privacy policy is updated
- Status codes: 0=no_record, 1=active, 2=revoked

## Known Issues / Design Notes

1. **Counter overcounting**: `grantConsent` increments `total_consents_granted` on every call, including re-consents from existing subjects. This is intentional — the counter tracks consent *operations*, not unique subjects. For unique subject count, use off-chain indexer queries on `consent_status`.

2. **block_number Uint<16> limit** in DataAuditLog: supports up to 65,535 blocks (~45 days at 1 block/min). For long-lived deployments, pass epoch-relative block offsets, or upgrade to Uint<32> in a future version.

3. **No access control**: any caller can log events in DataAuditLog. For production, this should be restricted to authorized controllers only. Compact 0.29.0 doesn't have built-in ACL; solution is to maintain an `authorized_controllers: Map<Bytes<32>, Uint<8>>` and assert membership before writes.

4. **purposes bitmask is not verified on-chain**: the contract stores whatever Uint<8> is passed. Invalid combinations (e.g., `0xFF`) are accepted. Caller is responsible for passing valid bitmasks. Input validation is best done in the TypeScript layer.
